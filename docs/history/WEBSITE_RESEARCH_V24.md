# Peach State Recruits: website research and upgrade plan

Prepared November 20, 2026. Research covers the supplied source archive, the current public homepage, official recruiting/education resources, official college athletics pages and competing platforms’ own documentation. Recommendations are product judgments informed by those sources; they are not measured conversion lifts or guarantees of recruiting outcomes.

## The main decision

Build the site around **helping an athlete take the next useful recruiting action**. Keep the media service as a clear paid offer. A polished profile alone is weak differentiation: SportsRecruits already includes a profile, video tools and school research in its free account. Its documentation reserves additional messaging/tracking tools for Pro. That supports your earlier concern about charging mainly for a profile. [SportsRecruits account features](https://help.sportsrecruits.com/hc/en-us/articles/4411807530509-What-is-the-Difference-between-a-Free-and-Pro-Account)

Your useful combination is a baseball audience, hands-on media production, understandable evaluation context and a connected action plan. A family should quickly answer: “What should we prepare? Which schools deserve research? What should we say? Is this trip worthwhile? What would college actually cost?”

## Why these improvements matter

### 1. Preparation should lead to a real conversation

The NCAA recruiting overview emphasizes division/sport differences and student ownership of communication in later high-school years. Scranton’s baseball page shows a real program asking for a questionnaire, video and an upcoming schedule. Those are useful preparation tasks the site can help an athlete finish. Neither source establishes a universal promise that submitting information produces recruitment. [NCAA recruiting overview](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/recruiting/) · [Scranton baseball recruiting instructions](https://athletics.scranton.edu/sports/2023/5/9/baseball-recruiting-info.aspx)

The roadmap connects preparation to existing tools instead of requiring a new account first. Keep Email Coach athlete-led: accurate facts, a specific school reason, an accessible video link and a clear evaluation question. The existing writer already does much of this; the new entry points make it easier to use.

### 2. Academic readiness is more than overall GPA

The NCAA distinguishes approved core-course work and core-course GPA from a general transcript GPA. The site should direct families to counselor review, the approved-course list, transcripts and eligibility-account tasks. It should not declare an athlete eligible from a single GPA field. The new plan makes this actionable without claiming certification. [NCAA initial eligibility requirements](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/initial-eligibility-requirements/)

NAIA has its own pathway and resources. JUCO questions should go to the college and the relevant current association rules; the directory’s JUCO grouping includes different associations. Do not treat every junior college as operating under one identical rulebook. [NAIA prospective athletes](https://www.naia.org/student-athletes/prospective/) · [NJCAA compliance resources](https://www.njcaa.org/compliance)

### 3. A recruiting calendar needs two separate checks

The current DI manual confirms baseball’s general August 1 beginning-of-junior-year communication boundary and separate September 1 boundaries for specified contacts/visits. Annual calendar restrictions still apply. Nontraditional calendars, transfers, signed prospects and other exceptions need school compliance review. [2026–27 DI Manual, bylaws 13.1, 13.4, 13.6, 13.7 and 13.12](https://s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/sidearm.nextgen.sites/ualbanysports.com/documents/2026/9/15/NCAA_Division_I_Manual_2026-27.pdf)

The official calendar includes November 24–29 and December 22–27, 2026 shutdowns. A shutdown restricts recruiting correspondence and calls as well as visits/evaluations; it is different from a dead period. The new module applies overrides to broader calendar periods, shows source links and returns “calendar needs checking” outside its reviewed season. It does not silently reuse this season’s dates in future years. [Official 2026–27 baseball calendar](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/calendar/2026-27/2026-27D1Rec_MBARecruitingCalendar.pdf) · [NCAA shutdown education, February 2026](https://ncaaorg.s3.amazonaws.com/compliance/recruiting/d1/D1Rec_Shutdown.pdf)

### 4. Camp spending needs a decision process

Use the camp worksheet to ask what is being purchased: development, exposure to specified staff, or a requested evaluation. Confirm the listed staff’s actual participation and the athlete’s permitted class/position. Calculate registration, travel, lodging and other costs together. Unknown fees stay incomplete.

The recommendation that a broad invitation alone does not prove recruiting interest is an inference: open registration and promotional distribution do not establish a coach’s individual assessment. The NCAA manual regulates open camps; individual program guidance describes multiple evaluation channels. A personal request is useful context but still not a roster offer. [DI Manual, camp provisions in 13.12](https://s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/sidearm.nextgen.sites/ualbanysports.com/documents/2026/9/15/NCAA_Division_I_Manual_2026-27.pdf)

### 5. Financial comparison should show price before financing

Use the same academic year and housing assumptions. Add tuition/mandatory fees, housing/meals and remaining costs, then subtract grants and scholarships once. Loans finance the remaining cost; work-study requires earnings. Estimated aid and written offers should be labeled separately. The worksheet also invites renewal/source notes rather than assuming four years of identical aid. [Federal Student Aid: evaluating financial aid offers](https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/)

DIII does not offer athletic scholarships, but families may still qualify for other aid. Do not describe it as “no aid.” [NCAA Division III eligibility guidance](https://www.ncaa.org/eligibility-center/initial-eligibility-requirements/division-iii/)

### 6. Views should not become invented recruiting interest

Apple documents background loading of remote email content independent of engagement. Consequently, an image-open signal cannot verify who read a recruiting email. Even SportsRecruits explains that its verified-coach profile views can represent several kinds of evaluation. Keep actual replies, requests, calls, visits and written terms separate from views. This update adds guidance; it does not claim to add inbox sync or verified coach tracking. [Apple Mail Privacy Protection](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/) · [SportsRecruits view explanations](https://help.sportsrecruits.com/hc/en-us/articles/11386857141133-Understanding-View-Notifications)

FieldLevel’s own explanation also highlights a current coach’s role in evaluating and promoting athletes. A future coach-reference workflow could be valuable, provided it records real permission and real feedback rather than manufacturing endorsements. [FieldLevel: athlete and coach roles](https://support.fieldlevel.com/en/articles/872248-athletes-vs-coaches-roles)

These are sequencing judgments, not measured revenue forecasts. Do not add AI offer probabilities, inferred roster openings, guaranteed scholarships, copied coach emails or automatic mass outreach as substitutes for reliable data and athlete judgment.

Responsive iframe checks verify CSS layouts, not native iOS Safari behavior.

No production deployment or live database changes occurred.
